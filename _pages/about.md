I am a researcher working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, health data science, and human behavior, with strong focus on compuational models and AI-driven personalized health interventions that help us understand what works, for whom, and under which circumstances. Much of my research has explored how advanced modelling, dynamic data collection, and real-time analytics can enable personalised lifestyle guidance and adaptive interventions. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, health data science, behavioural psychology, and neuroscience, with a strong focus on uncovering meaningful patterns across diverse data streams— ranging from mobile sensing and wearables to behavioural, socioeconomic, and psychosocial determinants.

I completed my PhD at the Vrije University of Amsterdam, The Netherlands, where my work centered on understanding human health behavior usingAI mainly focusing on building computational and AI-driven models to understand and influence health behavior change, integrating behavioral, neuropsychological, and contextual data sources.

Currently, I work as a postdoctoral researcher on large-scale, interdisciplinary health and data science projects, including EU-funded consortia and collaborations within Amsterdam UMC. My work spans health technologies, machine learning pipelines, wearable data, and cloud-based research infrastructures. I am particularly interested in translating complex data into actionable, personalized insights that can guide prevention, diagnostics, and behavior change.

My broader goal is to bridge advanced AI methods with health science to create scalable, meaningful technologies that improve people’s lives — especially in preventive care, ersonalized health guidance, improving adherence to interventions, and supporting healthier lives in real-world settings.
